Energy Is One of Your Largest Production Costs. Why Is It Not a Daily KPI?

 
 

Manufacturing organizations rigorously track operational performance.
Every day, teams review:

•OEE
•Throughput
•Downtime
•Quality
•Production Targets
 

These metrics drive decisions on the shop floor and directly influence profitability.

 

Yet one of the largest contributors to production cost—energy—is often reviewed only through monthly utility reports and consumption summaries.

 

By the time inefficiencies become visible, the opportunity to act has already passed.

The Missing KPI in Manufacturing Excellence

 
 

Most plants today have no shortage of energy data.
Meters are installed.
Dashboards are available.
Reports are generated.

 

But energy often remains disconnected from operational decision-making.
As a result, critical questions remain difficult to answer:

•Which process is consuming the most energy per unit produced?
•Why did specific energy consumption increase this week?
•Which equipment is operating inefficiently?
•Where are the biggest opportunities for improvement?
•How does one production line compare with another?

Without this visibility, energy becomes a reporting metric instead of a performance metric.

 

The Shift Leading Manufacturers Are Making

 
 

Forward-looking manufacturing organizations are beginning to manage energy with the same discipline used for productivity and quality.

 

They are moving from:

Monthly Energy Reports → Continuous Energy Intelligence
Consumption Tracking → Performance Benchmarking
Historical Analysis → Real-Time Decision Support
Utility Monitoring → Manufacturing Excellence

The focus is no longer just on measuring energy.
The focus is on understanding how energy impacts production performance, operational efficiency, and manufacturing cost.
